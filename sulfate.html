Sulfate: a fingerprint of the bacteria that make methylmercury

Sulfate-reducing bacteria are the main mercury methylators in low-oxygen water, and they use up sulfate as they work. So where the bottom water has less sulfate than the surface, bacteria have been active. This page compares that “sulfate drawdown” with oxygen and methylmercury.

2 · More sulfate used, more methylmercury

3 · At the thermocline: algae peaks vs bacteria peaks

Mid-depth (metalimnion) bottles. In oxygenated water sulfate matches the surface (no bacterial sulfate reduction) while chlorophyll is high; in low-oxygen water sulfate is drawn down and MeHg is several times higher.

Surface (EPI) and bottom (HYP) sulfate grab samples on the same day at the deep station, matched to that day’s bottom-water DO and hypolimnion MeHg (2005–2026). A surface–bottom sulfate difference can also come from water masses with different sulfate (inflows, imported water); sulfide measurements would confirm bacterial reduction. Iron-reducing bacteria and methanogens also methylate mercury and do not consume sulfate.
